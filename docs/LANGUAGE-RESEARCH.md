# Language profile research ledger

Reviewed 1 November 2026. This is an editorial verification record for maintainers, not content shown during play. Public profiles are original summaries under the AISOD Corpus presentation. Vocabulary provenance and licence metadata remain separate. No current speaker totals are asserted: comparable, variety-specific census counts were not established.

## Oshikwanyama

- University of KwaZulu-Natal, *The story of the Bible among Ovakwanyama: the agency of indigenous translators*: https://researchspace.ukzn.ac.za/items/6c1ea12a-b306-4d7b-b808-3c9512d18709 — northern Namibia/southern Angola and the role of indigenous translators.
- *Order out of chaos: Mandume ya Ndemufayo and oral history*, Journal of Southern African Studies: https://doi.org/10.1080/03057079308708349 — Kwanyama history and Mandume's place in oral historical memory.
- University of Turku, *The Contribution of Finnish Missionaries Towards the Development of Oshiwambo Language and Culture*: https://sites.utu.fi/intertwined-histories/the-contribution-of-finnish-missionaries-towards-the-development-of-oshiwambo-language-and-culture/ — Oshiwambo varieties, written standards, literacy and local writers.

## Oshindonga

- University of Turku, *Omandongo*: https://sites.utu.fi/intertwined-histories/omandongo-the-site-of-the-forsaken-mission-station-and-a-reminder-of-the-plights-of-pioneering-times/ — Ondonga's institutions and the 1870 missionary encounter; does not treat local actors as passive recipients.
- National Library of Finland, *Namibia Collection*: https://www.kansalliskirjasto.fi/en/collections/namibia-collection — 1876 alphabet book and 1901 Oniipa printing press.
- NIED first-language syllabuses: https://www.nied.edu.na/documents/syllabuses/seniorsecondary/Syllabuses/ — distinct Oshindonga and Oshikwanyama educational standards.

## Otjiherero

- Oxford Guide to the Bantu Languages, *Otjiherero (R30)*: https://academic.oup.com/book/61440/chapter-abstract/534774683 — distribution in Namibia, Angola and Botswana.
- UNAM Press catalogue, *Otuzo twOvaherero*: https://www.unam.edu.na/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/UNAM-PRESS-Catalogue-2023.pdf — patriclans, folklore, praise poems and communities sharing Otjiherero.
- SOAS, *The form and meaning of Otjiherero praises*: https://soas-repository.worktribe.com/output/408363/the-form-and-meaning-of-otjiherero-praises — oral poetry as historical and linguistic practice.
- UNESCO, *Teaching to prevent atrocity crimes*: https://www.iicba.unesco.org/sites/default/files/medias/fichiers/2023/09/Teaching%20to%20prevent%20atrocity%20crimes-%20a%20guide%20for%20teachers%20in%20Africa.pdf — Herero and Nama genocide, 1904–1908. No casualty estimates copied into the app.

## Khoekhoegowab

- *The Khoisan Languages of Southern Africa: Facts, Theories and Confusions*: https://doi.org/10.1080/02560046.2019.1647256 — language classification and naming; avoids treating “Khoisan” as one language family.
- Menán du Plessis, *Kora*, introduction: https://sahistory.org.za/archive/kora-lost-khoisan-language-early-cape-and-gariep-menan-du-plessis-introduction — collective naming and the Nama variety in South Africa.
- UNESCO, ancestral musical knowledge assistance record: https://ich.unesco.org/en/assistances/aixan-gana-b-ans-tsi-khasigu-ancestral-musical-sound-knowledge-and-skills-01418 — Nama/Damara communities, regions and musical transmission.
- UWC, *A study of dialectal and inter-linguistic variations of Khoekhoegowab*: https://uwcscholar.uwc.ac.za/items/d95ceca3-e8c3-46cf-9f01-aadbcc68e329 — collective name, dialects and orthography.

## Chokwe

- Felix Banda, Stellenbosch Papers in Linguistics PLUS 38 (2009): https://spilplus.journals.ac.za/pub/article/download/50/90 — explicitly notes Chokwe is also spoken in Namibia. Namibia is included without inventing a current count or claiming a particular Namibian regional majority.
- UNESCO, *Sona, drawings and geometric figures on sand*: https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/sona-drawings-and-geometric-figures-on-sand-01994 — living Lunda Cokwe heritage, storytelling, memory and learning.
- Metropolitan Museum of Art, *Mwanangana playing a sanza*: https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/316173 — nineteenth-century chiefdoms, court art, oral history and Chibinda Ilunga. The profile does not reproduce object photographs or catalogue prose.

## Rukwangali

- Karsten Legère, *Language in Africa* 3(1): https://iling-ran.ru/library/languageinafrica/3/LiA_3_1_1_Legere.pdf — Nkurenkuru–Rundu/Cuangar distribution, radio and schooling.
- Kavango East Regional Council: https://kavangoeastrc.gov.na/regional-profile — Kwangali and Mbunza communities; distinctions from other Kavango languages.
- NIED syllabuses: https://www.nied.edu.na/documents/syllabuses/juniorprimary/Syllabus/ — contemporary educational use.

## Editorial boundaries

These profiles describe languages and living communities, not the import process. History is not reduced to colonial events; oral traditions and present-day creativity are included. Regional summaries are broad, and do not imply that a language is exclusive to an ethnic group or geographic boundary. Corpus contributors should be able to refine these summaries; a native-speaking editorial review remains useful, and is not claimed to have happened during this change.
